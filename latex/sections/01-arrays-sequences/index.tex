\section{Arrays \& Sequences}

Arrays are the foundational data structure in most algorithmic problems.
They provide constant-time indexed access and strong cache locality, but
require careful reasoning about indices, bounds, and in-place mutation.

This chapter focuses on recognizing array-based problem patterns and
maintaining clear invariants over contiguous memory.

\subsection{Core Ideas}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item Index-as-state reasoning
  \item In-place mutation vs auxiliary storage
  \item Prefix and suffix accumulation
  \item Simulation of elementary operations (e.g.\ addition, carry propagation)
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Common Pitfalls}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item Off-by-one errors at boundaries
  \item Unintended data overwrites during in-place updates
  \item Assuming sorting or uniqueness without verifying constraints
  \item Over-allocating space when a single pass suffices
\end{itemize}
